# Hero AgentX throughput on sixteen and thirty-two GB200s

## Question and decision criteria

Find the causes of Hero's low AgentX throughput and validate a faster BF16 serving recipe, comparing single sixteen- and thirty-two-GPU groups. Keep the step-144000 export, tokenizer, all 393 pinned sessions, AgentX 1.0.12, 262,144-token window, seed 42 and preset replay controls fixed. Use short diagnostics to select candidates, then full-hour AgentX runs at matched loads and enough traffic to expose a throughput knee. A configuration gain and a gain from higher offered load are separate results.

The current goal permits up to 32 GB200s at interactive priority and 32 at batch priority, 64 total and 32 per experiment. Use the currently verified spare NVL72 rack, preserve Hero training, and verify actual nodes and physical UUIDs after admission or replacement. Count setup, idle time, unused allocated GPUs, failures and validation in GPU-hours. The quotas are ceilings. Interactive priority may evict batch work; idle capacity is not required. Hero pretraining remains excluded. Avoid intrusive profiling during full-hour confirmation. Keep mutable caches separate and release unused groups.

Compare pinned upstream vLLM Router against native session affinity, then screen 2K/4K/8K/16K scheduler budgets adaptively. Compare one sixteen-GPU group with one thirty-two-GPU group at the same total offered load before increasing load. Preserve the complete native corpus, replay, warmup and validity gates. Publish focused draft PRs and leave every PR unmerged.

ShortConv forces CUDA graphs to NONE; compilation remains enabled.

The earlier 571 output tokens/s/GPU result used 3,072 clients that immediately started a new request when one finished, roughly 149 input / 767 output tokens and 256 sequences/rank.

#+begin_abstract
Marin Snowball 67B-A2B v0.1, a mixture-of-experts chat model, does not know its own name. With no
system prompt it says it is ChatGPT (thinking on, 46 of 52 identity prompts) or Nemotron (thinking
off, 41 of 52). Interpretability runs and a scan of the SFT data agree on the cause: the training
data never names the model, about 16k rows teach a Nemotron identity, and late routed experts fill
the gap with "generic AI assistant". This report covers every fix we tried in two phases. In the
first phase, we tried a default system prompt, an earlier identity SFT and weight merges of it,
rank-1 edits, steering vectors, MEMIT, a cold-start SFT, context distillation (CD) from v0.1 with an
identity system prompt as its own teacher, CD restricted to late attention, on-policy distillation
and two CD variants with tool replay. A precision check before this phase found five numerical
mismatches between the Levanter training stack and the vLLM serve, the worst being silent MoE token
dropping. None of these fixes taught the model its name without a prompt: no trained run said
"Marin Snowball" in more than 2 of 52 prompts (6 with thinking on), and the first CD run cut tool
calling by 20 points. In the
second phase we ran short SFTs from v0.1 on a community identity dataset mixed with replay of v0.1's
own answers. The main finding is that identity rows rendered with thinking on teach the model to
answer and stop instead of calling a tool: BFCL tool calling fell from 27.6% to about 15%, whether
the thinking span was empty or held real reasoning. Rendering identity rows with thinking off, with
a 10% share of thinking-on rows with reasoning and the thinking-on tool-calling replay doubled (arm
A13), gives a model that never claims another lab with thinking on (0 of 52), keeps BFCL at 28.3%
(+0.7 [−3.4, +4.4]) and loses 1.1 GSM8K points. A13 is the lead candidate; A3 (thinking-off identity
rows only) is the fallback.
#+end_abstract

* Introduction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:intro
:END:

Snowball v0.1 is the chat release of Marin's 67B-A2B mixture-of-experts model. Asked who it is,
with no system prompt, it answers as ChatGPT when its thinking channel is on and as Nemotron when
it is off. Its published chat template carries a default system prompt that fixes most answers, but
the project wants the model itself to know what it is, because downstream users strip or replace
system prompts, and because a model that invents a "system message" in its reasoning behaves oddly
in other tasks.

The goal, stated at the start, was a model with no system prompt that names itself and its creator,
denies being another lab's model, keeps third-person facts about other labs intact, and loses
nothing measurable on tool calling, maths and coding. The target identity changed once during the
work: the first phase trained toward "Marin Snowball, created by Open Athena"; the second phase
used a community identity dataset whose answers say "Marin's Latest MoE, developed and trained by
the Marin Community". Counts in each phase use that phase's target.

** Terms
- *Thinking on / off.* Snowball has a reasoning channel delimited by special tokens. "Thinking on"
  means the model writes reasoning before its answer. Agent harnesses run with thinking on.
- *Replay.* Rows where v0.1 itself wrote the answer to a prompt. Training on them keeps the model
  near v0.1 on that kind of prompt.
- *Context distillation (CD).* The teacher is v0.1 with an identity system prompt. The student is
  v0.1 trained on the teacher's answers with the prompt removed.
- *BFCL-Parity.* 123 tool-calling tasks run by an agent harness with thinking on. It is the tool-calling
  gate in both phases.
- *Updates.* One optimizer step on a global batch of 8 sequences × 8,192 tokens.

** The two phases in one paragraph
Phase one (2026-10-20 to 10-28) diagnosed the cause, tried every fix cheaper than a full SFT,
repaired the training stack, and then ran context distillation and three follow-ons. Every trained
run learned the creator but not the name, and the first run broke tool calling. Phase two
(2026-10-29 to 11-03) switched to a community identity dataset and ran thirteen short SFT arms
plus four merges, each scored on the same gates. Figure [[fig:diagnosis]] shows the diagnosis and
the phase-one fixes; Figures [[fig:data]] and [[fig:gates]] show the phase-two data and gates.

We located the wrong name with a torch reference harness that matches vLLM on every name and
creator decision, using logit lens, direct logit attribution, activation patching and
max-activation labelling of neurons.

#+ATTR_LATEX: :align p{4.3cm}p{5.2cm}p{6.3cm} :booktabs t
| Component                        | Role                                   | Evidence                                                  |
|----------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Routed experts, layers 16–23     | push " Chat" over " Marin"             | logit lens: ≈0 at layer 14 to −10.4 at layer 25           |
| Routed experts, layers 20 and 23 | push "AI" over "-Athena" after "Open"  | attribution −2.9 and −5.6; "AI" wins by 15.6 nats         |
| Shared expert, layers 24–25      | final push toward ChatGPT              | attribution −2.0, −2.9                                    |
| L20 expert 46 #1072, L23 expert 25 #1014 | top "wrong name" neurons       | same activation on self, third-person and neutral prompts |
| Heads L23H1, L23H4, L22H5, L22H3 | copy an identity from a system prompt  | patching recovers 0.32 / 0.26 / 0.24 / 0.15 of the gap    |

Three things follow. The top neurons store general facts about AI assistants, not a self-identity;
editing them would damage facts about other labs. A system prompt works by a copy mechanism in
late attention heads, not by changing what the experts store. And with thinking on, the model often
reasons "The system says: You are ChatGPT", inventing a system prompt that is not there.

The SFT data explains why. In a sample of about 497k of the 15.2M SFT rows, no row names Marin,
about 16k rows train a Nemotron identity on purpose, and about 2.2k rows carry ChatGPT asides in
the thinking channel. Pretraining data was not scanned.

* Measuring identity and its cost
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:measure
:END:

Every fix was scored on an identity screen and on a set of capability gates. The screens evolved
between the phases, so numbers are comparable within a phase and within a table, not across them.

- *Identity screen.* 52 core identity prompts, thinking off and on, no system prompt, plus
  third-person controls (14 in the development set, 30 unseen) that ask about other labs' products.
  Phase one counted "says Snowball" and "says Open Athena". Phase two counts name, creator and
  "claims another lab", and reads answers with special tokens kept so reasoning and answer split at
  the model's own end-of-thinking token. Only 31 of the 52 core prompts ask for the model's name;
  17 ask about the creator, origin or contact, where naming the creator alone is correct.
- *Teacher-forced gate (phase one only).* Negative log-likelihood per answer token of the target
  name and creator strings. Every trained run passed it by a wide margin, and it predicted nothing
  about generation (Section [[#sec:lessons]]).
- *BFCL-Parity.* 123 tool-calling tasks run with the Pi agent, thinking on. The agent sends no
  temperature, so the server default (1.0) applies, and single runs swing by 10 points (one
  checkpoint scored 29 and then 17 of 123). Phase two scores five full runs per model with a
  run-level bootstrap against a fresh five-run v0.1 baseline. Phase-one numbers are single runs and
  carry about ±10 points of paired noise.
- *Maths.* GSM8K (thinking off) and MATH500 (thinking on), paired against v0.1, 3 decodes each,
  item-level bootstrap. Pre-set stop line: a drop of more than 1.5 points with the whole interval
  below zero.
- *SWE-bench r100.* 100 SWE-bench Verified tasks with mini-swe-agent 2.1; only runs with all 100
  tasks scored count.
- *Panel-60.* An external 60-question identity and history panel, thinking off, temperature 0,
  graded blind by one model grader. Ship line: clean ≥ 20, name ≥ 15, claims another lab ≤ 3.
  Each phase ran its own grading pass, so the two v0.1 rows differ.
- *14-eval suite.* MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, HumanEval+, MBPP+, MATH500, GSM8K, SWE-bench r100,
  TBLite r100, BFCL and others; used for the system prompt, v0.1.1 and CD-16 only.

Two measurement facts shaped the design. The served model is not deterministic: vLLM with data and
expert parallelism changes 5.6% of its own expert choices between identical requests, and its
log-probs vary by up to 0.67 nats per answer, so each gate scores several passes. And the torch
reference changes routes with batch shape, so the Levanter-vs-serve agreement rule was set against
the measured floor (95.8% batched route agreement) rather than an ideal.

* Fixes without full training
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:cheap
:END:

** Default system prompt
The published chat template already carries "I am Marin Snowball, an AI model created by Open
Athena". With it, v0.1 answers 41 of 47 identity prompts correctly with thinking off and 35 of 47
with thinking on, and shows no cost on the 14-eval suite. Its one measured side effect is identity
leaking into fiction (8 of 47 story prompts). It is a mitigation, not a fix: the weights are
unchanged, and the goal is for the model to know its identity without a prompt.

** An earlier identity SFT and merges of it
An earlier identity SFT (v0.1.1) names itself in 48 of 48 prompts but loses 21 points of MMLU-Pro,
29 of HumanEval+ and 28 of GSM8K. Part of its drop is answer format (no =\boxed{}=, unfenced code).
A merge ladder v0.1 + α (v0.1.1 − v0.1) in BF16 finds no sweet spot: identity and the GSM8K loss
rise together.

of the delta at α = 0.5, which is why identity is unchanged there. Grafting only v0.1.1's attention
tensors into v0.1 (layers 18–23, or all layers) taught nothing in generation.

* Fixing the training stack first
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:stack
:END:

Before any full-model training, a zero-learning-rate precision check compared Levanter's Snowball
forward pass with vLLM's on the same inputs. It took eight 32-H100 runs plus small diagnostics
(108 H100-hours) and found that Levanter was computing a different model from the one being served.

#+ATTR_LATEX: :align p{4.4cm}p{6.6cm}p{4.8cm} :booktabs t
| Where                        | Mismatch                                                    | Fix                     |
|------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------|
| MoE dispatch                 | capacity factor 1.0 silently drops tokens; pad tokens dispatched unmasked | dropless MoE + pad mask |
| MoE combine                  | combine weights cast to BF16; vLLM keeps FP32               | FP32 combine            |
| XLA silu / sigmoid (13 sites) | one BF16 step off in 30–36% of elements                    | FP32 activations flag   |
| FlashAttention-4 core        | rounds the attention probabilities differently from torch; 30% of elements differ | torch-order attention |
| HF reload                    | silently dropped the attention implementation setting       | preserved and tested    |

The token drops were the main mismatch: dropless MoE moved top-1 token agreement with vLLM from
88.6% to 99.4% and the p99 log-prob error from 3.96 to 0.60 nats. The check also found twelve
plumbing bugs, each of which crashed a run: a multi-host mesh layout, a storage-path kwargs leak,
three multi-host gather bugs, an int32 narrowing of memory statistics, an export audit that rejected
the tokenizer saver's own chat template, a loss path that differed between training and the gate,
and others. With all fixes, the final check passed: one update ran, peak memory was 50.2 of 59.4
GiB, and all 502 exported tensors were byte-equal to v0.1.

Two of these matter beyond this project. Levanter's default MoE capacity factor of 1.0 drops
tokens silently under expert parallelism (now configurable), and the training loss path must be
the same code the gate uses.

* Context distillation and its follow-ons
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:cd
:END:

** CD-16: the first full-model run
The teacher is v0.1 with the system prompt "I am Marin Snowball, an AI model created by Open
Athena". The student trains on the teacher's answers with the prompt removed. The mix is 220
identity rows plus 547 replay rows (v0.1's own answers on new maths, code, chat and fiction prompts)
plus persona and third-person controls; identity is about 3.8% of supervised tokens. 16 updates at
learning rate 2e-6 on 32 H100 (24.7 H100-hours). Attention q/k projections, router weights and
biases and the 256 special-token rows are frozen; this freeze is kept in every later arm unless
noted.

- *Teacher-forced gate:* name NLL 8.85 → 1.45 nats per token, creator 14.02 → 0.011.
- *Generation:* names Open Athena in 38 of 52 prompts but itself in only 2 of 52 (6 with thinking
  on). It credits 4 of 30 unseen OpenAI products to Open Athena.
- *14-eval suite:* within noise of v0.1 except BFCL (−20.3 points; 109 of 123 trials end with no
  answer file), TruthfulQA (−1.4) and TriviaQA (+2.4). The training mix had no tool-calling rows.

The model and its training data (every row, the training order, mixture settings, QC verdicts and
generation scripts) are public (Section [[#sec:artifacts]]).

** Controls: late attention only
Training only q/k/v/o in layers 18–23 (24 tensors) on the same data kept tool calling but taught
almost nothing: at learning rate 1e-5 it names Open Athena in 14 of 52 prompts (thinking off only)
and never names itself, while its teacher-forced NLL moved almost as far as CD-16's. The identity
cannot be written into the copy heads alone.

** On-policy distillation from CD-16
Starting from CD-16, 20 steps of on-policy distillation with v0.1 + system prompt as the teacher on
identity and tool prompts, and plain v0.1 elsewhere (learning rate 3e-6; 51 H100-hours including
export). It repaired most of the tool calling (BFCL 18.7%; the no-answer failure is gone), but it
never names itself (0 of 52, 0 of 60 on the panel) and MATH500 fell 2.3 points. It was stopped.

** Tool replay and new identity rows (T2, T3)
One v0.1 serve produced 156 rows of v0.1's own tool-calling answers on prompts disjoint from BFCL,
112 new identity rows (name-first, and "Are you X?" denials) and 54 third-person controls. History
questions were meant to point at the project site, but the teacher invented dates and founders in
every answer, so no history row passed the filters.

T2 ran the CD-16 recipe on the 1,089-row pool. Tool calling recovered (22.8%), but 16 updates of
batch 8 sample only 128 sequences, and only 8 of the 112 new identity rows were seen. T3 reweighted
the same pool (new identity rows 4.5×, controls 6×, tool rows 3×), so 28 new identity rows, 19
controls and 25 tool rows were seen. BFCL rose to 35.0% (+7.3 vs the system prompt; CI −2.4 to
+17.9) and maths held, but the name count was 1 of 52, and panel claims to be another lab's model
rose from 5 to 10.

** What phase one taught
- Teacher-forced NLL does not predict generation. Every run passed the gate; none said its name.
- The creator is learned far more easily than the name, and it spreads: "My name is Open Athena",
  and other labs' products credited to Open Athena.
- A little tool replay prevents the tool-calling collapse: 25–31 tool rows in 16 updates were
  enough to go from 7.3% to 35.0%.
- Small SFT runs see little of their data. With 128 sequences per run, the sampling weights decide
  which rows train at all; T2 never saw the rows it was meant to test.
- A weight change small enough to keep every capability did not teach the name. The name may need
  more rows, a cleaner target, or identity data earlier in training.

* SFT on the identity dataset
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:pathb
:END:

Phase two changed the data rather than the method. Instead of distilling v0.1's prompted answers,
each arm is a short SFT from v0.1 on a community identity dataset
([[https://huggingface.co/datasets/marin-community/identity-data][marin-community/identity-data]]
at =665ac6e=, by Will Held) mixed with replay. The target identity becomes "Marin's Latest MoE,
developed and trained by the Marin Community".

** Data
- *Identity rows.* The dataset's question–answer pairs about name, creator and origin, after
  capping the "not known / should not guess" refusal rows. Each row can be rendered with thinking
  off (answer only) or thinking on (a reasoning span, then the answer).
- *Reasoning for thinking-on rows.* v0.1 itself cannot write it: with an identity system prompt
  as the teacher, 0 of 200 pilot traces passed QC because the reasoning quotes the system prompt,
  even when told not to; with A3 as a forced-thinking teacher, 11 of 200 passed. GLM wrote the
  reasoning instead; 1,791 identity rows were eligible for a GLM span, and ineligible rows fall
  back to thinking off.
- *Replay rows.* v0.1's own answers: a pool of 547 general rows (chat, code, maths, fiction,
  third-person) and 156 tool-calling rows, each usable with thinking off and on. A 36-update arm
  consumes about 340 chat, 279 code, 346 maths and 282 tool-calling rows (82 first calls, 113
  after a tool result, 87 final replies), plus a few fiction and third-person rows.
- *QC and packing.* Rows are rendered with the v0.1 chat template and no system prompt. Each
  source row is used at most twice across the two modes. The packed training file rebuilds byte
  for byte from the preserved inputs (SHA256 =f860030d= for A13).
- *Mix.* A 36-update arm consumes about 7,300–7,400 rendered rows. Identity rows are short, so
  they are most of the rows but about 10% of supervised tokens (A3 9.7%, A13 9.9%); replay rows are
  the rest. For comparison, CD-16's identity share was 3.8%.

36 updates from v0.1 (18 in A12, 72 in A9 and A11), learning rate 2e-6 constant, global batch
8 × 8,192 tokens, seed 310, on 32 H100. The q/k projections, router weights and biases and the 256
special-token rows are frozen except in A1.

The pattern is in the first column. Every arm whose identity rows are rendered with thinking on
(A0, A1, A6, A8b, A12) scores about 15–17%. Arms whose identity rows are thinking off (A3, A11)

does not help (A6 ≈ A0). A4, trained for 36 updates on the phase-one T3 data, has the best BFCL
(a single run, so not comparable with the five-run rows) and names itself in 42 of 52 prompts with thinking off, which the same
data could not do in 16 updates. It was a control only: it credits the old identity ("Marin
Snowball / Open Athena"), it rewrites other labs' facts in 5 of 14 controls (the worst of any arm),
and its identity read predates the fixed parser.

*** Training length versus tool-example dose
A12 is A8a stopped halfway, not a lower dose. The two kinds of "more" behave differently:

#+ATTR_LATEX: :booktabs t
| What increased                | Arms                                | BFCL                             |
|-------------------------------+-------------------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Training length, same mix     | A12 (18) → A8a (36) → A9 (72)       | 14.8% → 26.2% → 24.6%            |
| Thinking-on tool replay       | A8a (1×) → A13 (2×)                 | 26.2% → 28.3% (+2.1 [−1.5, +5.7]) |

Tool calling drops early in training and recovers by update 36; more training of the same mix does
not improve it further, and 72 updates add a few other-lab rewrites ("GPT-4 trained by the Marin
Community"). More thinking-on examples that end in a tool call help a little, consistent with the
answer-and-stop mechanism; the dose was not tested beyond 2×.

*** What did not help
- Self-distillation from v0.1 for thinking-on rows: 0 of 200 traces passed QC (Section [[#sec:pathb]], Data).
- More training (A9, A11): no identity gain, a few rewrites.
- Less training (A12): tool calling collapses.
- A tiny Tülu-style set (A10): weaker identity; still claims another lab in 9 of 52 thinking-on answers.
- Linear merges of A3 and A8a: BFCL and the system-message quirk switch together near t = 0.5, so no
  blend gets both A3's BFCL and A8a's clean reasoning.

* Lessons across both phases
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:lessons
:END:

1. *The data decided, not the method.* Context distillation, on-policy distillation and the
   identity-dataset SFT all use the same optimizer, learning rate and freeze. The phase-one runs
   taught the creator and not the name because the teacher's own answers rarely said the name
   cleanly and because 16 updates saw few of the rows that did. The dataset rows say the name in
   most answers, and 36 updates see all of them.
2. *Tool calling breaks through the thinking channel.* Both the phase-one collapse (no tool rows at
   all) and the phase-two collapse (thinking-on identity rows) are the model learning to end a
   thinking-on turn without a tool call. The repair is the same both times: thinking-on replay
   rows that end in a tool call, in enough quantity.
3. *The loss gate does not see any of this.* Teacher-forced NLL passed in every run; BFCL and
   generation counts were the only measures that moved. A paired agentic tool-use eval belongs in
   every SFT screen.
4. *Thinking-off-only identity training leaves a reasoning quirk.* The model reasons about a system
   message that is not there. A small share of thinking-on rows with real reasoning fixes it, and
   the share has a narrow window (10% works, 30% collapses tool calling).
5. *Length is not monotonic.* Tool calling collapses at 18 updates and recovers at 36; checking one
   checkpoint can mislead.
6. *Measure with enough passes.* The serve is non-deterministic, and BFCL at temperature 1.0 swings
   by 10 points between runs. Five full runs with a run-level bootstrap was the minimum that made
   arm differences readable.
7. *Fix the stack first.* The precision check cost 108 H100-hours and found a silent token-dropping
   bug that would have made every later result uninterpretable.

** Limitations
- Panel-60 is a development set graded by one model, not by people. Only the within-phase
  comparisons are blind.
- The two phases use different identity targets, different screen readers and different v0.1
  serves, so their counts are not directly comparable.
- The GSM8K cost of the 36-update recipes (1–2 points) is unexplained; the replay mix has not been
  varied.
- Mini840 and a full release check of A13 are pending; no broader capability suite was run on the
  phase-two candidates beyond maths, BFCL and SWE-bench r100.
- Pretraining data was not scanned for identity rows.

* Conclusions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sec:conclusions
:END:

A13 is the lead candidate. Its recipe is identity rows rendered with thinking off, plus a 10% share
of thinking-on identity rows with GLM-written reasoning, plus the thinking-on tool-calling replay
rows doubled, trained for 36 updates from v0.1. With thinking on it names itself in 35 and its
creator in 32 of 52 core prompts and never claims another lab (v0.1: 46 of 52). BFCL is 28.3%
(+0.7 [−3.4, +4.4] vs v0.1), GSM8K −1.1 [−1.9, −0.3] and MATH500 unchanged. A3 (thinking-off rows
only) is the fallback: best BFCL (31.1%), but a system-message quirk in 17 of 52 reasoning traces
and a GSM8K drop (−1.7) that trips the stop line. The earlier recommendation to rely on the default
system prompt is withdrawn: the identity now lives in the weights.

Open work: a release check of A13 on the full suite, the pending Mini840 run, and a replay-mix
sweep aimed at the 1–2 point GSM8K cost.

** Cost
Phase one used 530.5 H100-hours and 319 CPU-hours across 245 jobs (precision check 108, CD-16 142,
late-attention and merge controls 78, on-policy distillation 135, T2 and T3 62, panel serves 5).
Phase two used at least 3,168 H100-hours and 1,154 CPU-hours through 2026-10-31 (training and
exports 2,166, evals 708, data generation 45, other 249); A12, A13, the merges and their evals ran
after the ledger closed and are not booked. Five-run BFCL on every arm is most of the eval cost.
These are cluster allocations, not provider billing, and exclude sandbox time for the agentic evals
and the shared GLM serve.

RUBRIC: Identification of Tumor-Specific Enriched Cell Subpopulations via Tissue Distribution Analysis in Colorectal Cancer

Total Points: 100/100

Notes: Full-score answers demonstrate a rigorous, biologically grounded tissue-specificity analysis that systematically quantifies the distributional preference of each cell subtype across three anatomically distinct compartments, tumor tissue, normal adjacent tissue (NAT), and peripheral blood (PBMC). The gold-standard approach uses the Ratio of Observed to Expected (Ro/e) framework based on chi-square expected frequencies, but proportion-based fold-change methods that correctly normalize for unequal tissue sampling are also valid. The analysis is rooted in the immunological principle that the tumor microenvironment (TME) harbors unique cell subpopulations shaped by chronic antigen exposure, hypoxia, metabolic stress, and tissue-specific cytokine milieus, populations that are absent or rare in normal tissues and the peripheral circulation. A full-score answer: (1) restricts analysis to baseline (Treatment='I') to avoid treatment-induced redistribution confounding and filters for the three tissue compartments; (2) constructs a count or proportion table of cell subtypes across tissues; (3) computes a tissue-enrichment metric that accounts for unequal sampling depth (e.g., Ro/e via chi2_contingency, or proportion-based fold-change normalized by tissue size); (4) applies a tumor-specificity filter requiring enrichment in tumor AND depletion/lower representation in both normal tissue and blood; (5) correctly identifies the top tumor-specific cell types including c64_Mph_SPP1, c79_Fibro_FAP, c70_Endo_COL4A1, c23_CD8_Tex_LAYN; and (6) interprets these findings in the context of tumor-associated macrophage immunosuppression (SPP1+), cancer-associated fibroblast remodeling (FAP+), T cell exhaustion (LAYN+), and the translational potential for tumor-specific therapeutic targeting with minimal off-target toxicity.

RUBRIC: DA-15-7

Total Points: 100/100

Notes: This rubric evaluates transcriptome-wide gene expression correlation with disease duration as a continuous variable to identify progression biomarkers. Excellence requires loading data from three spinal cord regions (cervical, lumbar, thoracic) with ALS patients having disease_duration information, implementing proper normalization (quantile + TMM), fitting linear models with disease_duration as predictor while controlling for technical and biological covariates using voom + limma framework, applying Benjamini-Hochberg multiple testing correction, identifying significant genes primarily in cervical spinal cord with both positive and negative correlations, reporting top correlated genes with CHIT1 as strongest negatively correlated gene, and validating CHIT1-duration relationship with Spearman correlation showing strong negative correlation (p < 0.001) indicating shorter disease duration associates with higher CHIT1 expression.

CRITERIA (6):

Criterion 1: Data Loading, Cohort Selection, and Study Design

    Description: Assesses correct data loading, filtering to appropriate ALS cohort, and understanding of continuous duration analysis.
    Levels: A=15 B=8 C=0
      [A]: Correctly loads gene expression data (counts and TPM) and metadata from all three spinal cord regions (Cervical, Lumbar, Thoracic), filters metadata to include only ALS patients (disease == "ALS") with non-missing disease duration information (!is.na(disease_duration)) and RIN values (!is.na(rin)) ensuring quality control, converts disease_duration to numeric continuous variable for correlation analysis (not categorical bins), clearly documents that analysis treats disease duration as continuous predictor of gene expression (longer duration vs shorter duration), reports sample sizes after filtering for each tissue, and explains research goal is identifying genes whose expression levels correlate with progression rate measured by disease duration.

      [B]: Loads data with issues such as missing one or more tissues (only analyzes cervical without lumbar/thoracic), incomplete filtering (includes patients with missing disease_duration or RIN causing errors in modeling), treats disease_duration as categorical variable (fast vs slow progressors) instead of continuous which changes analysis type from correlation to group comparison, unclear documentation of cohort selection criteria making it difficult to verify correct filtering, does not report sample sizes after filtering, or study design explanation confuses disease duration with other phenotypes (age at onset, survival, case-control status).

      [C]: Fails to load correct datasets (uses wrong tissues, missing gene expression or metadata files), does not filter to appropriate cohort (includes non-ALS samples, or excludes most ALS patients with incorrect filtering logic), fundamentally misunderstands research question (performs case-control DE analysis instead of duration correlation, or analyzes wrong phenotype entirely), data loading is so incomplete that transcriptome-wide correlation analysis cannot proceed, or no valid cohort is defined for analysis.

Criterion 2: Gene Filtering, Normalization, and Statistical Model Specification

    Description: Evaluates proper gene filtering, normalization procedures, and linear model specification with appropriate covariates.
    Levels: A=20 B=10 C=0
      [A]: Applies gene filtering to remove lowly expressed genes using TPM threshold (e.g., requiring expression > 1 TPM in at least 50% of samples to ensure reliable measurements), performs quantile normalization to reduce technical variance across samples followed by TMM (trimmed mean of M-values) normalization for library size differences, specifies linear model with disease_duration as primary predictor of interest while controlling for relevant covariates including biological factors (sex), technical quality metrics (rin, rin_squared for non-linear RIN effects, pct_mrna_bases for library quality), batch effects (library_prep, site_id for sequencing site), and genetic ancestry (gPC1-gPC5 for population stratification), uses voom transformation to convert counts to log2-CPM with precision weights accounting for mean-variance relationship in RNA-seq data, fits linear models using limma framework which is appropriate for continuous outcome analysis, and clearly documents model formula and rationale for covariate inclusion.

      [B]: Performs analysis with issues such as gene filtering unclear or too stringent/lenient (removes too many or too few genes affecting power or noise), normalization incomplete (missing quantile normalization or TMM, or uses only one method), model specification missing key covariates (excludes RIN, batch effects, or genetic PCs which could confound duration associations), does not account for mean-variance relationship (skips voom transformation causing inappropriate variance estimation), uses inappropriate statistical framework (simple correlation without covariate adjustment, or wrong regression model type), unclear documentation of model formula making it difficult to verify correct specification, or model has technical issues that partially affect results but overall approach recognizable.

      [C]: Does not filter genes appropriately (uses all genes including non-expressed causing noise, or removes expressed genes incorrectly), does not normalize data (uses raw counts without normalization leading to technical bias), does not specify appropriate statistical model (performs simple t-tests, uses wrong model type, or no regression framework), model excludes disease_duration as predictor (cannot identify duration-correlated genes), does not control for any covariates (confounded by batch, sex, quality differences), or statistical approach is so incorrect that duration-gene associations cannot be validly assessed.

Criterion 3: Transcriptome-wide Association Testing and Multiple Testing Correction

    Description: Assesses correct genome-wide statistical testing and proper multiple testing correction.
    Levels: A=25 B=12 C=0
      [A]: Performs transcriptome-wide differential expression analysis testing association between disease_duration coefficient and gene expression across all expressed genes (testing thousands of genes in each tissue after filtering), applies empirical Bayes moderation to t-statistics using eBayes function to borrow information across genes and improve statistical power (critical for moderate sample sizes), calculates two-sided p-values for each gene testing whether duration coefficient differs from zero, applies Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) false discovery rate correction to control for multiple testing across all genes tested, defines significance threshold using adjusted p-value (adj_p_val or FDR) < 0.05, correctly identifies significant genes in each tissue finding that Cervical Spinal Cord shows substantial number of significant genes (including both positively and negatively correlated with duration) while Lumbar and Thoracic Spinal Cord show minimal or no significant associations, and interprets that cervical spinal cord is the primary tissue showing transcriptomic associations with disease duration while other regions lack statistical power or biological signal.

      [B]: Performs testing with issues such as tests subset of genes instead of transcriptome-wide (arbitrary filtering reduces scope), does not apply empirical Bayes moderation (uses standard t-tests without information borrowing reducing power), applies wrong multiple testing correction method (Bonferroni too conservative, or no correction), uses unadjusted p-values for significance calls (does not properly control false discovery rate), significance threshold unclear or inappropriately stringent/lenient, reports substantially different pattern of significant genes across tissues suggesting methodological issues, does not analyze all three tissues (missing lumbar or thoracic), or testing framework has technical issues affecting results but overall approach recognizable.

      [C]: Does not perform transcriptome-wide testing (tests only handful of candidate genes, or no systematic testing), does not apply any multiple testing correction (reports only unadjusted p-values with no FDR control leading to high false positive rate), uses wrong statistical test (correlation without covariates, t-test instead of regression, or inappropriate non-parametric tests), significance calls are incorrect or fabricated (reports wrong numbers of significant genes inconsistent with analysis), does not test disease_duration coefficient (tests wrong model terms or no duration associations), or statistical testing is so flawed that gene-duration associations cannot be reliably identified.

Criterion 4: Results Reporting and Gene Ranking

    Description: Evaluates correct reporting and ranking of top positively and negatively correlated genes.
    Levels: A=20 B=10 C=0
      [A]: Reports results separately for each tissue focusing on cervical spinal cord as primary tissue with significant findings, ranks genes by log fold-change (or effect size) representing magnitude of association between gene expression and disease duration, clearly identifies and reports top negatively correlated genes (genes with negative log_fc indicating expression decreases with longer duration, or equivalently higher expression in shorter duration cases) including CHIT1 as strongest negatively correlated gene, followed by other immune-related genes, clearly identifies and reports top positively correlated genes (positive log_fc indicating expression increases with longer duration), reports essential statistics for top genes including gene names, log fold-changes showing effect magnitude and direction, adjusted p-values demonstrating statistical significance after multiple testing correction, and presents results in organized format (table or ranked list) with clear distinction between positive and negative associations.

      [B]: Reports results with issues such as does not clearly separate positive vs negative correlations (reports all genes together without directional distinction), ranking unclear or inconsistent (not sorted by effect size, or ranking criteria ambiguous), fails to identify CHIT1 as strongest negatively correlated gene (CHIT1 missing from top list, or incorrectly ranked below other genes), reports gene names but missing essential statistics (no log fold-changes, missing adjusted p-values, or unclear effect directions), incomplete reporting (provides top genes for only one direction, or lists genes without clear ranking), does not distinguish cervical results from other tissues (reports combined results obscuring tissue-specific patterns), or reporting format is unclear making it difficult to identify which genes are most strongly correlated with duration.

      [C]: Does not report top correlated genes appropriately (no ranked list, or reports only p-values without effect sizes), reported genes are incorrect or inconsistent with analysis (fabricated results, cherry-picked genes not actually significant, or wrong genes listed), does not identify CHIT1 at all (completely missing from results despite being strongest negatively correlated gene), does not distinguish positive from negative associations (reports only one direction, or direction information missing), reported statistics are wrong or nonsensical (log fold-changes inconsistent with p-values, or values outside plausible ranges), or results reporting is so incomplete or incorrect that top duration-associated genes cannot be identified.

Criterion 5: Biological Validation and Interpretation of CHIT1

    Description: Assesses validation of CHIT1-duration relationship and biological interpretation in ALS context.
    Levels: A=20 B=10 C=0
      [A]: Performs validation analysis specifically for CHIT1 by calculating Spearman correlation between CHIT1 expression (log2 TPM) and disease duration across cervical spinal cord ALS patients, reports Spearman correlation coefficient showing strong negative correlation with highly significant p-value (p < 0.001), correctly interprets direction explaining that negative correlation means higher CHIT1 expression associates with shorter disease duration (faster progression) while lower CHIT1 expression associates with longer duration (slower progression), provides biological interpretation explaining CHIT1 (chitinase-1) is marker of microglial/macrophage activation and neuroinflammation in ALS, notes that elevated CHIT1 in fast progressors suggests stronger inflammatory response associates with more aggressive disease course consistent with glial activation as driver of ALS pathology, contextualizes finding within broader results noting other immune/glial genes also correlate with duration, and avoids overstating by acknowledging correlation does not prove causation or determine whether CHIT1 elevation is consequence or cause of rapid progression.

      [B]: Validates CHIT1 with issues such as performs correlation but uses Pearson instead of Spearman (less robust to outliers), reports correlation coefficient but does not show expected strong negative correlation pattern, does not report p-value or statistical significance of correlation, interprets direction incorrectly or unclearly (confuses whether higher CHIT1 means faster or slower progression), biological interpretation is generic or shallow (mentions "CHIT1 is important" without explaining chitinase function, glial activation, or inflammation), does not connect CHIT1 finding to ALS pathobiology (missing discussion of neuroinflammation or disease mechanisms), or interpretation lacks nuance about correlation vs causation.

      [C]: Does not validate CHIT1-duration relationship with correlation analysis (no validation performed beyond initial regression), reports incorrect correlation (positive correlation when should be negative, or magnitude/significance grossly wrong), does not identify CHIT1 as strongest negatively correlated gene (claims other genes are stronger or misses CHIT1 entirely), provides incorrect biological interpretation (misidentifies CHIT1 function, claims opposite relationship to progression than data shows, or biologically implausible conclusions), makes unsupported causal claims inconsistent with observational correlation data, or provides no meaningful biological interpretation connecting CHIT1 to ALS progression biology.

Criterion 6: Source Reliability

    Description: Evaluates whether the agent grounds its values, identifiers, and biological / clinical interpretations in identifiable sources, the provided data files, named public databases, or real peer-reviewed references, rather than asserting facts from memory without source.
    Levels: A=0 B=-5 C=-10
      [A]: Numerical results are traceable to the provided data files or to a documented transformation. Identifiers (gene / protein / sample / pathway names) come from named sources (HGNC, UniProt, MSigDB, KEGG, the dataset's own feature index). Statistical method names match what was actually run. Biological / clinical interpretations are supported by real, identifiable references (author + year + DOI / PubMed) rather than free-floating assertions.

      [B]: Most numerical results and identifiers are traceable, but some interpretive claims are asserted without source attribution or cite only vague phrases like "studies show".

      [C]: Reports values, identifiers, or biological claims with no clear source. Hallucinated content (made-up identifiers, fabricated statistics, invented citations) mixed with computed results.

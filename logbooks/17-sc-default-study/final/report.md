# Snowball score-centering default comparison

Keep score centering disabled by default and keep its candidate-count default at 128. The bounded study is complete at five matched seeds per arm, fifteen fifty-update runs. Every comparison is inconclusive against the agreed one-percentage-point quality and 5% cycle-time limits. Point estimates favor SC32, but the decision intervals allow harm beyond both limits. No material regression is established either. These findings support retaining the current settings pending Romain's decision; they do not show that 128 is empirically better than 32. Product defaults and commits are unchanged.

Score centering (SC) subtracts the sampling policy's expected clipped score from the policy-update gradient. For current policy p, stored pre-update policy o and sampling policy q, that score is the log-probability gradient multiplied by the advantage, truncated o/q weight, p/o ratio and PPO's directional clipping mask. The expectation is exact on the captured candidate IDs. Outside this head, q and o are modeled as rescaled copies of p that preserve their measured tail masses. SC32 and SC128 use 32 and 128 captured candidates. The [implementation](../../../vendor/skyrl/skyrl-train/skyrl_train/objective/score_centering.py) gives the exact formula.

The primary result is SC32 versus ordinary tokenwise truncated importance sampling (TIS). The two supporting comparisons are SC128 versus TIS and SC32 versus SC128. Snowball is the frozen RL task pool spanning calendar, competitive coding, Reasoning Gym puzzles, STEM and structural tasks. Each family has quality weight 0.2. Full task success means the original normalized verifier score is at least 1−1e−6; partial-credit answers do not count as full successes. Quality is the equal-family mean of that indicator on the same 9,989 held-out tasks. Lower cycle time is better. Two of the eleven common task exclusions were approved after outcomes were known. Quality intervals combine variation across the five paired training seeds with variation across held-out tasks within each family. They use a Student t critical value with four degrees of freedom and retain the seed–task interaction conservatively. Cost intervals use paired seed log time ratios, then exponentiate. Tokens and updates are not treated as independent runs. The two-look adjustment gives marginal coverage for each metric/comparison under that model; it gives no joint guarantee across all comparisons. The reported cost centers are geometric. Ratios of arithmetic mean times are −11.32%, +5.93% and −16.28%, respectively.

This conclusion is limited to one model, this data mixture and a fifty-update window with five matched seeds.
